# Expression learning: separate human and synthetic feedback evidence

Baseline tests exposed that a PROJECT_SYNTHETIC TOO_TERSE counterexample could veto human-tagged evidence, and that synthetic review volume could outvote one human review. An active lesson originating in synthetic experience could also be presented with human qualification. The change groups support and counters by origin, ignores UNREVIEWED feedback, prioritizes human-tagged reviews when present without allowing one review to satisfy the two-case threshold, and requires the original lesson experience's origin to match the qualifying group at testing and at policy projection. Human counterexamples can veto a synthetic hypothesis, but synthetic counterexamples cannot veto human evidence. All sources, Store authority and unrelated factual memory remain unchanged.

100 relevant tests passed, including original S8 and dialogue; bound synthetic local exchanges/reviews showed later different-turn context change across restart. Tests use fixtures with USER_PROVIDED_REVIEW labels, not real human testimony or proof of delivery. Baseline failure logs are retained. First integration fixture failed because a local renderer treated the wire request as context; it was fixed to parse context_json and rerun passed. No real model, production, phone, human naturalness or token savings claim. More specific per-memory mentionability, confirmed-delivery attribution and broader learning remain open.
